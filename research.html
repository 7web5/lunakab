03 / RESEARCH

Research

Across 10 articles, 6 patents and 10 previous solutions, one gap kept appearing: the pieces of lunar cargo logistics exist, but never together. Cranes like LSMS and LANDO can offload a lander but stay in place; rovers like Pegasus, CLV-1 and FLEX can travel but need astronauts to load and direct them; and autonomous navigation works, but so far only for science missions. With a five-second signal delay ruling out driving from Earth, and sharp dust and radiation making every walk outside a risk, we set out to build one robot that does the whole job alone — a horizontal crane to load itself, a sealed bay and conveyor to carry and hand off cargo, four-wheel skid steering to turn and dock, and front sensors to find its own way.

EBSCO · 2024 · By Karunya
Source

Cargo spacecraft | EBSCO. (2024). EBSCO Information Services, Inc. | www.ebsco.com.

ebsco.com/research-starters/engineering/cargo-spacecraft ↗
Artifact summary

It defines what cargo spacecraft are: they are designed to carry supplies into orbit. Cargo spacecraft were first made by scientists who worked for the Soviet Union in the 1960s. They created the TKS, a Transport Supply Spacecraft, but this required a pilot and crew. In order to maintain a permanent crew, however, the ISS needed a steady stream of supplies going in and out. The European Space Agency later created the Automated Transfer Vehicle, which could carry around 7,600 kg, and the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency developed the H-II Transfer Vehicle, which could carry around 6,000 kg of material. The ATV and HTV were unmanned, autonomous/remote-controlled, and launched from Earth. After the Space Shuttle retired, SpaceX launched the Dragon, the first reusable cargo spacecraft. Engineers are continuing to improve on these designs.

Artifact critique

This artifact talks about the differences between the cargo spacecraft developed over the years, but doesn't mention critiques of any of them. I would like to learn more about how the Dragon could be further improved and what scientists are looking for in future spacecraft. As of right now, it seems that most of these spacecraft are launched from Earth and then attach themselves to the ISS, and humans have to retrieve the cargo — so an autonomous cargo transport vehicle between the supply building and the payload seems to be what engineers are trying to build.

Reliability

The article references around 10 sources, all from recent NASA articles, and was itself published around 2020. With all this information, I believe the source is reliable, as it uses direct research from NASA and is factual rather than biased.

Source

Toronto, U. (2025, October 18). Researchers' autonomous system makes it easier to transport cargo on the moon. Phys.org.

phys.org/news/2025-10-autonomous-easier-cargo-moon ↗
Artifact summary

This article describes how a team of researchers at the University of Toronto Institute for Aerospace Studies realized that the distance between a cargo lander's landing site and the astronauts' habitat was too far. Even though gravity is 1/6 of Earth's, making the cargo lighter, the distance between the sites poses a greater threat than the weight of the cargo. The landing site is flat for a safe arrival, whereas the habitat is shielded behind rocky terrain, which means astronauts would have to walk across that terrain to retrieve the cargo and bring it back. Instead of having humans do it, the researchers are building a rover that works autonomously to retrieve cargo from the landing site and deliver it to the habitat. Previous rovers were built to explore the lunar surface, whereas this rover is built to travel back and forth along the same path. The article also covers technical challenges they faced, such as developing a new semi-autonomous "teach" approach, since regular joystick control wouldn't work with the five-second delay between command and feedback.

Artifact critique

Since this article relates directly to my project of creating an external cargo robot, I learned more about the significance of this robot and the problems that may come up (such as developing better autonomous technology) while working on it. It also helped me understand that researchers are actively working on this problem, so there is a lot of room for improvement with these new rovers.

Reliability

This article was published on Phys.org, which is generally considered credible, as it often links to peer-reviewed research and is part of the Science X network. However, much of its content relies on university press releases, which could exaggerate a study's claims.

SOLUTION 01

Astrolab · By Karunya
Source

FLEX Rover, Astrolab: About – Astrolab

astrolab.space/about ↗
Product summary

It is a multi-purpose lunar vehicle that can carry people, cargo, and science equipment. It can pick up, carry, and deposit modular payloads of up to 1,600 kg with more than 3 m³ of usable payload volume. It has a robotic arm that can reach around 2 meters and is designed to manipulate payloads and perform logistical tasks such as interacting with objects on the surface, deploying equipment, or assisting with science operations. Instead of a conventional fixed suspension, it has limbs with wheels at the ends, which let the rover handle uneven terrain and lower or raise itself. It has autonomous navigation capabilities, with navigation and hazard-detection sensors. Overall, this robot has a solar power system and communication equipment, can carry two astronauts, can assist with scientific and logistics experiments, and can carry cargo from the payload lander to the habitat.

Product critique

It can carry a lot of load, but its limit is 3 m² and 1,000 kg, and our mission is to create a robot that can carry up to 20 ft cargo modules. Additionally, this robot has not been tested in space yet; it is a conceptual prototype that has been tested in California, at NASA Johnson Space Center, and at other lunar analog sites.

It has six articulated limbs, each with a wheel at the end. Essentially, the structural idea of this robot — especially the six limbs — is influential, but it lacks the degree of autonomy our vehicle will need to exhibit.

NASA · Mars 2020 · By Karunya
Source

Perseverance, NASA: Perseverance Rover Components - NASA Science

science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-2020-perseverance/rover-components ↗
Product summary

The Perseverance rover is a NASA Mars rover that landed in Jezero Crater in 2021. Its main purpose is to study the Martian surface and search for signs of ancient microbial life, and it collects rock and soil samples. The vehicle is a six-wheeled rover that uses cameras, sensors, and an autonomous navigation system called AutoNav to detect obstacles, create maps of the terrain, and plan safe routes. Our external cargo transport project could implement a navigation system similar to AutoNav — one that continuously updates a map of the lunar surface as it moves — but the overall missions of the two vehicles are completely different.

xWalker - Enhanced Inspection and Manipulation for Lunar Assembly, NASA

Product summary

It is an advanced autonomous robotic framework designed to support large-scale infrastructure on the lunar surface. It also has high-dexterity manipulation, letting it grasp complex objects and handle sensitive payloads with extreme precision. Our robot will have to carry much heavier and larger loads, using a crane-like robotic arm to carry them.
